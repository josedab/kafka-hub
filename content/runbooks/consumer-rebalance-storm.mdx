## Symptom

Consumer group generations/epochs advance repeatedly, members repeatedly join
and leave, partition processing pauses, commits fail, duplicate exposure rises,
and lag follows a sawtooth pattern. A scale-out or rolling deploy can trigger
the first event, but a continuing storm means the group cannot settle.

Do not fix this by blindly adding members. A hot partition remains one
partition, and every extra restart can produce more churn.

## Quick checks (in order)

1. Measure rebalance frequency and correlate each event with member churn,
   deploys, restarts, autoscaling, node drains, GC pauses, and downstream
   timeouts. Good: one expected event around a controlled change. Bad: a new
   generation/epoch every few minutes or faster.
2. Describe group membership and per-partition lag:
   ```bash
   kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server "$BS" --describe --group "$GROUP"
   ```
   Good: stable members with assigned partitions and falling lag. Bad: missing
   members, rapid assignment changes, or one member/partition never progresses.
3. Compare worst-case poll-to-complete time with `max.poll.interval.ms`.
   Good: processing, retries, and GC complete well inside the interval. Bad:
   `CommitFailedException`, poll-timeout logs, or a blocking model/database call
   consumes the interval.
4. Check `session.timeout.ms` and heartbeat behavior against observed GC/network
   pauses. Good: no expiry during normal tail latency. Bad: the coordinator
   evicts healthy-but-paused members.
5. Identify the active group protocol and assignment behavior:
   - classic eager vs classic cooperative-sticky;
   - KIP-848 consumer protocol (`group.protocol=consumer`) with broker-side
     assignment and group/member epochs.
   Do not call all three “cooperative”; they have different coordination paths.
6. Check deployment identity and static membership. Good: one logical member
   owns one `group.instance.id` and rollout order is controlled. Bad: duplicate
   static IDs, readiness probes that kill slow consumers, or a Deployment
   replacing all members at once.
7. Check consumer handler/downstream latency and partition skew. Good:
   heartbeats and processing both progress. Bad: a process stays in the group
   but its worker/executor is wedged, or one hot partition dominates lag.

## Likely causes

- **`max.poll.interval.ms` exceeded** — tell: long handler batches, blocking
  calls, and commit failures occur just before reassignment.
- **Session/heartbeat expiry** — tell: GC/network gaps align with member
  eviction despite otherwise healthy processing.
- **Crash-loop or deployment churn** — tell: pod lifecycle events and group
  churn share timestamps.
- **Static membership collision/misuse** — tell: duplicate instance identities
  or stale members prevent a predictable rollout.
- **Classic eager assignment behavior** — tell: every membership change pauses
  all partitions, amplifying lag even when only one moves.
- **Protocol migration mismatch** — tell: some clients/configs do not support
  the intended classic cooperative or consumer-protocol path.
- **Hot partition/downstream stall** — tell: the group remains joined but one
  owner makes no progress and scaling cannot split its key.

## Fixes

### A slow poll loop or blocking handler

Reduce records per poll, move bounded concurrent work behind the poll loop, and
set `max.poll.interval.ms` above measured worst-case processing plus recovery
margin. Do not increase it to hide indefinitely stuck work; add a watchdog
that fails a wedged process so ownership can move.

```properties
max.poll.records=100
max.poll.interval.ms=900000
```

### Deployment or crash churn

Roll back the bad release or suspend autoscaling until the group reaches one
stable generation/epoch. Restart one member at a time, wait for assignment and
lag to stabilize, then continue. Use readiness checks that distinguish a
temporary downstream slowdown from a dead consumer.

### Static membership

Make `group.instance.id` unique per concurrently running logical member and
stable across a planned restart. Do not share a single value across Deployment
replicas. Test expiration/recovery before relying on it during a broad rollout.

### Assignment/protocol choice

For classic clients, cooperative-sticky can reduce unnecessary partition
movement compared with eager assignment. It is still a classic protocol.
KIP-848's consumer protocol uses a broker-side coordinator and incremental
reconciliation; validate client/broker support and migration order in a
non-production group before changing production `group.protocol`.

### Hot partition or slow dependency

Scaling members cannot split work from one partition. Fix the key distribution,
isolate the tenant/workload, or improve the downstream dependency. Pause only
affected partitions when appropriate and keep the consumer heartbeating.

## Safe rollback / stop

- Do not change assignment strategy, group protocol, static membership, and
  deployment topology in one rollout.
- Stop a rollout when rebalance frequency rises or duplicated side effects
  appear; restore the prior known-good client config/member count.
- Treat a consumer crash as at-least-once work. Verify idempotent downstream
  effects before forcing a member replacement.

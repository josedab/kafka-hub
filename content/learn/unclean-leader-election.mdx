The ISR protocol guarantees that if you produced with `acks=all`, your data
lived on at least `min.insync.replicas` brokers before the leader acked. The
moment **every** replica in the ISR is dead, Kafka has a hard choice: stay
unavailable until one of those replicas comes back, or hand leadership to a
known-stale replica and silently drop the unreplicated tail.

That choice is controlled by exactly one setting: **`unclean.leader.election.enable`**.

## The scenario

You have a partition with `replication.factor=3` and `min.insync.replicas=2`.
A short, plausible failure sequence:

1. Broker 2 GCs for 12 seconds — its lag passes `replica.lag.time.max.ms`,
   so the controller removes it from the ISR. ISR = `{0, 1}`.
2. Producer keeps writing. Offsets 10–14 land on the leader (broker 0) and
   replicate to broker 1 only.
3. Broker 1's disk fails. ISR shrinks to `{0}` — leader-only.
4. Broker 0 power-cycles. Now **no member of the last ISR is alive**. The
   only replica that responds to fetch requests is broker 2, which last saw
   offset 9.

## The trade-off, explicitly

| Mode                                   | What you keep                              | What you lose                          |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| `unclean.leader.election.enable=false` | Durability — never serve uncommitted data  | Availability until ISR recovers        |
| `unclean.leader.election.enable=true`  | Availability — partition comes back fast   | Any acked message past the stale offset|

In the demo above, the unclean path **silently drops 5 acked messages**.
The producer received an `acks=all` ack for those offsets. Downstream
consumers may have already processed messages with those offsets (because
the leader exposed them via the high water mark). When broker 2 takes over
at offset 9, those consumers either get the **new** offsets 10+ (different
messages with the same offset numbers — a correctness disaster) or skip
them entirely, depending on how the consumer was positioned.

## Default it to `false`

Apache Kafka's default since 0.11 is `false`. Override only if you have a
very specific reason — typically a single-DC, availability-over-durability
ingestion path where the data is replayable from an upstream system.

Confluent Cloud, Redpanda Cloud and AWS MSK all default to `false` and
recommend against changing it.

## What "wait for ISR" actually looks like

In the safe path, the partition is in **`Offline`** state. Producers get
`NotLeaderOrFollowerException`. Consumers either time out or block. Your
on-call alerting should fire on partition `Offline` count > 0, not on broker
liveness — broker liveness can be fine while a specific partition is dead.

Recovery is a sysadmin operation:

1. Bring back **any one** of the replicas that was in the last ISR
   (preferably the most-recent leader, broker 0 in our demo).
2. The controller will elect it leader and the partition is back at offset 14.
3. Broker 2, when it later recovers, will truncate its log to the leader
   epoch and refetch.

The `kafka-leader-election.sh` tool has a `--election-type UNCLEAN` mode if
you decide you want to force the unsafe election manually — that's a
deliberate, audited choice rather than a config that fires automatically
under stress.

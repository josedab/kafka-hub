When you hear a Kafka engineer say "the durability story" they almost always
mean the interplay between three settings: `replication.factor`,
`min.insync.replicas`, and the producer's `acks`. The behavior is determined
by a fourth, mostly invisible concept — the **in-sync replica set**, or ISR.

This article walks through what the ISR actually is, how it changes, and how
each `acks` value behaves under healthy and unhealthy conditions. There's an
embedded simulator below so you can drag a follower out of sync and watch
producers start failing.

## The ISR set

A topic-partition has `replication.factor` replicas. One of them is the
**leader**; the others are **followers**. Followers fetch from the leader.

A follower stays in the ISR set as long as it has fetched up to the leader's
log end within `replica.lag.time.max.ms` (default 10 seconds). If it falls
further behind — because the broker is GC'ing, the network is congested, the
disk is saturated, or the broker is just down — the controller removes it
from the ISR set. When it catches back up, it rejoins.

The high water mark — the offset producers and consumers care about — is the
**minimum log end** across the current ISR. That's why a slow follower stalls
your consumers even though the leader is fine.

## acks, in plain English

| acks  | Leader waits for                | Worst case loss                                        |
| ----- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| `0`   | nothing — fire and forget       | every message in flight                                |
| `1`   | leader's own log append         | one message, if the leader crashes before replication  |
| `all` | every replica currently in ISR  | zero, _if_ `min.insync.replicas` is set sensibly       |

The trap people walk into is setting `acks=all` and leaving
`min.insync.replicas=1`. The leader is then the only replica it has to wait
for, so you pay the latency of `acks=all` and get the durability of `acks=1`.

## Diagnostic rules that catch this in your config

The Diagnose surface ships two rules tied directly to this concept:

- **`acks-all-with-min-isr-1`** — flags the exact misconfiguration above
- **`replication-factor-too-low`** — warns when default RF is below 3

If you have a `server.properties` to hand, [run it through Diagnose](/diagnose).

## Symptom

`UnderReplicatedPartitions` is non-zero, a topic describe shows replicas outside
ISR, or an application sees `LEADER_NOT_AVAILABLE` / unavailable partitions.
These are related but not interchangeable:

- **Under-replicated partition (URP):** a leader exists, but one or more
  assigned replicas are not in ISR. Reads/writes may still work; durability and
  failure headroom are reduced.
- **Offline/unavailable partition:** no live leader can serve the partition.
  Reads and writes for that partition are unavailable until a safe leader path
  is restored or an explicit data-loss decision is made.

**Stop condition:** do not enable unclean leader election, delete replica
directories, or reassign away the only known in-sync copy just to make a
dashboard green. Escalate the data-loss trade-off with the topic owner first.

This runbook is reviewed against Apache Kafka 4.3.1. Confirm exact command
output and client tooling against the patch version you run.

## Quick checks (in order)

1. List the affected sets separately:
   ```bash
   kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server "$BS" --describe --under-replicated-partitions
   kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server "$BS" --describe --unavailable-partitions
   ```
   Good: the sets are empty or shrinking. Bad: unavailable partitions are
   growing, or URP spans multiple brokers/racks.
2. Describe each affected topic-partition and record leader, replicas, ISR,
   leader epoch, and the timestamp of the first alert. Good: a leader remains
   in ISR. Bad: ISR is empty or every replica is offline.
3. Identify the common failed broker/log directory/rack. Check broker process
   health, controller logs, disk/inode space, filesystem errors, replication
   fetch latency, and network loss before changing topic configuration.
4. Check `min.insync.replicas`, producer `acks`, and current produce errors.
   Good: `acks=all` producers fail safely below the minimum. Bad: availability
   pressure is pushing teams to weaken the durability requirement without a
   recorded decision.
5. Check whether a reassignment, broker replacement, or network maintenance
   coincided with the alert. Good: movement is progressing and has headroom.
   Bad: replicas are repeatedly leaving ISR or target disks are full.
6. For unavailable partitions, locate every replica's last known healthy
   storage. Do not format, delete, or force-start a replacement until the
   incident lead knows whether an in-sync replica can be restored.

## Likely causes

- **Broker or rack unavailable** — tell: the same broker/rack owns missing
  replicas, and controller/broker health shows a real outage.
- **Slow or broken disk/log directory** — tell: filesystem errors, high IO
  latency, offline log-directory logs, or no free space coincide with ISR loss.
- **Network/replication path degradation** — tell: replica fetch errors or
  lag rise while broker processes remain up.
- **Unsafe reassignment or capacity exhaustion** — tell: affected partitions
  match moving replicas and target brokers lack disk/network headroom.
- **No live replica for a partition** — tell: every assigned broker is down or
  unable to mount the relevant log directory; the partition is unavailable, not
  merely under-replicated.

## Fixes

### A broker or rack is temporarily unavailable

Restore the failed broker/rack path before moving replicas. Verify the broker
registers, its directories mount read-write, and follower fetch catches up.
Let ISR re-form before treating the incident as resolved.

```bash
kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server "$BS" --describe --topic "$TOPIC"
```

If the broker returns but a replica cannot catch up, investigate its disk and
network path rather than repeatedly restarting the process.

### A log directory is full or offline

Use [Disk full or log directory offline](/runbooks/disk-full-or-log-dir-offline)
to distinguish filesystem pressure from an offline replica store. Relieve
pressure with a bounded, policy-approved retention or reassignment action; do
not remove log segments manually.

### Replication is slow or network-degraded

Restore the replication path, then remove only temporary throttles that are
known to be constraining catch-up. Watch per-partition ISR, replica lag, disk
queue depth, and client latency together. A faster reassignment can worsen the
failure if it saturates the healthy brokers.

### No live replica can lead the partition

Treat this as a data-safety incident. Recover a known replica/storage path if
possible. If business owners explicitly choose unclean recovery, record the
affected topic-partitions, expected lost offset range, and validation plan
before changing the setting. After availability is restored, preserve evidence
and rebuild replication factor/ISR before declaring recovery complete.

## Safe rollback / stop

- Stop a reassignment if it reduces healthy-broker headroom or causes more ISR
  loss; cancellation does not undo replicas already moved, so replan from the
  observed state.
- Roll back a maintenance change only after checking that no new replicas were
  placed onto an unhealthy target.
- Never use unclean leader election as an automatic rollback. It is an
  acknowledged data-loss action.
